TikTok stopped being just an entertainment platform long ago, and TikTok advertising is one of the channels we are asked about most. Some companies get a noticeable share of their sales from it; others spend the budget and are disappointed. The difference is usually not in the ad account settings but in whether the channel suits the product and whether the business can produce content at the required pace.

Let's look at who TikTok ads are for, what a working campaign consists of, how to test and which signs show it is too early to start. The budget figures in this article come from TikTok Ads Manager help at the time of publication; the platform updates its rules regularly, so check them before launching.

## How TikTok differs from other ad channels

**Content matters more than targeting.** The algorithm finds people who are interested in a video based on their behaviour. A good video finds its audience even with broad settings; no settings will save a bad one.

**Ads have to look like regular content.** People scroll fast. A video that looks like an advert from the first second gets skipped. What works is vertical video in the platform's style: real people, real situations, fast pace, subtitles.

**Creative burns out quickly.** The same video tires the audience faster than in other channels. You need a steady stream of new versions.

**Demand has not formed yet.** Unlike search, a person on TikTok was not looking for anything. Advertising creates interest rather than answering a query, and it competes not with other advertisers but with all the entertainment content in the feed. So the path from view to purchase is often longer and needs additional touchpoints.

## Who it suits

TikTok works best when:

- **the product can be shown on video** — the result is visible: food, cosmetics, clothing, interiors, renovation, education, entertainment;
- **the purchase decision is quick** — a low or medium price point, buying online, booking a service;
- **the audience is on the platform** — your customers actually spend time there;
- **you have capacity for content** — in-house filming, creators, a production team or willingness to work with creators.

It is harder when the product is difficult to show, the sales cycle is long and the decision is made by a group, or when the brand cannot afford a lively, informal tone.

## Minimum budgets

According to TikTok Ads Manager help, with a daily budget the campaign budget must exceed 50 US dollars and the ad group budget must exceed 20 US dollars (accounts in other currencies have equivalents). With a lifetime budget, the minimum for an ad group is the number of days multiplied by the minimum daily budget.

The minimum is not a recommendation for a test. For the algorithm to learn and for you to draw conclusions, the budget must buy enough target actions. Work out the real amount from your economics: how much you are willing to pay for a customer and how many customers you need to know whether the channel works.

## Creative for the format

A TikTok ad that works usually:

- **grabs attention in the first seconds** — with a question, an unexpected shot, the result before the explanation;
- **is shot vertically** and looks like content from the platform's creators, not a TV commercial;
- **has subtitles** — many people watch without sound;
- **shows the product in use**, not on a white background;
- **ends with a clear action** — what to do next and why.

Formats that work well: a real person's review or reaction, before and after, unboxing, an answer to a common question, behind the scenes of production, a short how-to.

**Spark Ads** let you promote posts from a brand's or creator's account (with their permission) as ads. The video keeps its likes and comments and looks organic.

## Creators instead of in-house filming

Not every business can film many videos itself. The alternative is working with content creators who shoot videos in the platform's style. For a brand, it is a way to get native videos from real people.

How to organise it:

- **a brief without a word-for-word script** — the task, key messages, what must be shown and what to avoid; the rest in the creator's own style;
- **several creators at once** — different approaches quickly show which style works for your audience;
- **usage rights** — the contract with the creator states whether the video can be used in ads, on which platforms and for how long;
- **Spark Ads** — if the video is posted on the creator's account, it can be promoted with their permission while keeping its organic look.

## Moderation and restrictions

Every ad on TikTok is reviewed against the platform's advertising policies. Some categories of products and services are restricted or prohibited, and some topics have additional requirements. Before launching, check the rules for your category in TikTok's help centre: rejected ads and repeated violations slow you down and can lead to account restrictions. Also check the advertising laws of the country where the ads will run.

## Organic and paid together

TikTok ads work better when the brand has a living account. Someone who sees an ad often visits the profile, and an empty or abandoned account reduces trust. Organic posts are also a cheap way to test ideas: videos that do well without promotion are the first candidates for paid distribution.

## The funnel: one video is not enough

Because a person on TikTok was not looking for anything, the path to purchase often has several steps:

1. **Reach and interest.** Videos that introduce the product and make people want to know more.
2. **Engagement.** Repeat touches for those who watched to the end or interacted: more about the product, reviews, answers to objections.
3. **Action.** An offer for those who already know you: enquiry, purchase, booking, message.

For the funnel to work, the TikTok pixel must be on the website with events set up and, for accuracy, server-side event sharing. Without it, the algorithm cannot tell which impressions lead to purchases and optimises for views.

## How to test

1. **Define the goal and metric:** enquiries, purchases, bookings — not views or likes.
2. **Prepare several different creatives** — different ideas, not one video with different captions.
3. **Run the test with enough budget and time** for campaigns to exit the learning phase.
4. **Judge by cost per target action and by quality** — how many enquiries turned into sales.
5. **Scale gradually.** A sharp budget increase can send a campaign back into learning. TikTok's help centre gives guidance on how much to change budgets at a time — follow it.
6. **Refresh creatives on a schedule**, without waiting for results to drop.

## When it is better not to start

Signs that TikTok is not the right channel right now:

- **no capacity for regular video production** — the campaign will burn out in a couple of weeks;
- **the website or page is not ready for mobile traffic** — slow, awkward, no clear action;
- **analytics are not set up** — you cannot tell whether the ads bring customers;
- **the product is hard to explain in 15–30 seconds**, and there is no warm-up content;
- **no budget for a test** that can collect enough data;
- **the sales team is not ready** for a flow of enquiries from messengers and comments.

In these cases it is better to start with channels where demand already exists — search advertising, for example — and return to TikTok when you have capacity for content.

## Metrics that matter

- **Cost per target action** — enquiries, purchases, bookings.
- **Watch-through and retention** — how many people watch beyond the first seconds. Weak retention means the opening needs changing.
- **CTR** — the share of clicks after a view.
- **On-site conversion** — how many clicks turn into actions. High CTR with low conversion points to a problem with the page or a mismatch of expectations.
- **Cost per customer from CRM data** — the final check of whether the channel pays back.

Also account for the delayed effect. Someone may see a video on TikTok and buy later — after finding you in search or messaging you. So when evaluating the channel, look at growth in branded searches and direct enquiries during the campaign, and ask new customers where they heard about you.
